A(4) (the A above the middle C) is often 440 Hz, as adopted by the International Organization for Standardization (R16, 1975). [1]

A(3) is therefore 220 Hz
A(4) is 440
A(8) is 880

Therefore, the Hz difference between octaves is not always the same.

What is always the same is the mathematical relationship between 2 frequencies. For example, it is x2 for octaves.

A vibrating string produces sounds at different modes.

f1 = the frequency produced by the string at 1/1 its length
f2 = the frequency produced by the string at 1/2 its length
f3 = the frequency produced by the string at 1/3 its length
f4 = the frequency produced by the string at 1/4 its length
f5 = the frequency produced by the string at 1/5 its length
f6 = the frequency produced by the string at 1/6 its length

f1 is the fundamental note.
f2, 3, ... are overtones

f1 = fundamental
f2 = (fundamental x 2/1) octave
f3 = (f2 x 3/2)
f4 = (f3 x 4/3)
f5 = (f4 x 5/4)
f6 = (f5 x 6/5)
